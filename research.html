A note on “boys vs girls”

Developmental guidance at this age is not sex-specific — the CDC, ASQ-3 and WHO use the same milestones for every baby. Research differences are tiny and mostly appear later: a small early female edge on some fine-motor and gesture measures, slightly higher average activity levels in boys. Sex often explains only ~1–2% of the variation in early language, so it's no basis for individual expectations. Track Arlo against the standard milestones, and raise any single missed one with your clinician rather than putting it down to being a boy. One practical finding: adults sometimes talk and gesture a little more with girls — a nudge to be intentional about rich, gesture-filled chat with a son.

🚩

When to check in with a professional

Milestones describe what most babies do — not a pass/fail test. But talk to your health visitor or GP (and ask about developmental screening) if, by around 12 months, Arlo:

  • isn't babbling, gesturing (no waving or pointing), or responding to his name;
  • doesn't look for hidden objects or play back-and-forth games;
  • isn't sitting steadily, or isn't bearing weight / pulling to stand;
  • isn't using a thumb-and-finger (pincer) grasp; or
  • has lost a skill he previously had.

The AAP recommends routine developmental screening at 9, 18 and 30 months regardless of concerns. Use corrected age if he was born more than 3 weeks early.

🧭

Honest caveats

  • Milestones are population markers; healthy babies vary widely. The CDC sets them at the level ~75% of children reach (and deliberately moved away from “wait and see”).
  • The classic “30-million-word gap” (Hart & Risley, 1995; just 42 families) has been criticised for sample size and bias, and a larger study found a much smaller gap. The field has shifted toward interaction quality (Romeo; Hirsh-Pasek).
  • Piaget's ages for object permanence are debated — some experiments suggest babies understand it earlier than he thought.
  • Several support studies were done with slightly older toddlers or specific communities, so effects on the very youngest infants are less certain.
  • This page is educational and isn't a substitute for advice from Arlo's own clinician.
